How to Convert JPG to PDF and PDF to JPG Without Uploading

Turn photos and scans into one tidy PDF, or save PDF pages as high-quality images, right in Chrome. Free and private.

Shahriar AhmedDeveloper of Unique PDF · November 9, 2026 · 2 min read

Images and PDFs often need to change places. A landlord wants your documents as one PDF, but you have phone photos. A presentation needs a page from a PDF as a picture. Both conversions take seconds with Unique PDF, and your files never leave your computer.

Part 1: Turn images into a PDF

Images to PDF combines photos, scans and screenshots into a single document. It accepts the common formats you are likely to have, including JPG and PNG.

  1. Open Images to PDF from the Unique PDF quick menu or the Convert section.
  2. Add your images. Drag them onto the page or click Choose files. You can add more at any time.
  3. Put them in order by dragging the thumbnails or using the arrow buttons.
  4. Choose the layout: page size (such as A4 or Letter), orientation, margins, and whether each image fits the page or keeps its original size.
  5. Click Create PDF and download the result.

Why your photos come out the right way up

Phone cameras often store photos sideways and add a note telling viewers to rotate them. Many converters ignore that note and produce sideways pages. Unique PDF reads it and turns every photo the right way up automatically.

Quality without bloat

JPEG photos are placed into the PDF as they are, without being compressed a second time, so they keep their original quality. If the final file is too big to email, run it through Compress PDF afterwards.

Part 2: Save PDF pages as images

PDF to images exports pages as pictures.

  1. Open PDF to images from the Convert section.
  2. Add your PDF and select the pages you want, or keep all of them.
  3. Choose the format: PNG for sharp text and graphics, JPEG for photos and smaller files.
  4. Choose the resolution, from 72 DPI for screens up to 600 DPI for printing.
  5. Export. A single page downloads as one image; several pages download together as a ZIP archive.

Which resolution should I pick?

  • 72 to 150 DPI — websites, chat apps and slides.
  • 200 to 300 DPI — documents you might print or zoom into.
  • 600 DPI — detailed print work. Files get large quickly, so use it only when you need it.

Why convert in your browser?

Photos of IDs, receipts and signed forms are exactly the kind of files you should not upload to a random website. With Unique PDF the conversion happens on your own computer, with no limits on how many images you add and no watermark on the result.
