Sending a payslip, a contract or a medical report by email? Adding a password means that only people who know it can open the file, even if the email is forwarded or the attachment ends up in the wrong place.

Unique PDF protects PDFs with AES-256 encryption, the strongest standard encryption for PDF files, and it does so inside your browser.

## Step by step

1. **Open Protect PDF** from the Unique PDF quick menu or the Security section.
2. **Add your document.**
3. **Type a password** and type it again to confirm. A strength meter shows how strong it is.
4. **Set permissions (optional).** Open the Permissions section to decide whether people may print the document (in high or low quality, or not at all), copy text, change it, add comments or fill in forms.
5. **Click Protect PDF** and download the protected copy.

Anyone who opens the file will now be asked for the password.

## Choosing a good password

- **Use a passphrase.** Four or five unrelated words are long, strong and easier to remember than a jumble of symbols.
- **Do not reuse** a password from your email or bank.
- **Share the password separately.** Send the file by email and the password by text message or a phone call, never in the same email.

## What the permissions do (and do not do)

Permissions such as "no printing" or "no copying" are respected by standard PDF apps. They are useful for setting expectations, but they are not a strong security barrier, because some software ignores them. The open password is what really keeps the content private.

If you leave the permissions password empty, Unique PDF generates a random one. That way nobody can lift the restrictions just by knowing the open password.

## Keep your password safe

Unique PDF does not store your password and cannot recover it. If you forget it, the protected copy cannot be opened. Keep the unprotected original somewhere safe, or store the password in a password manager.

## Why protect PDFs in your browser?

Uploading a document to an online service in order to protect it means trusting that service with the unprotected version first. With Unique PDF, encryption happens on your own computer, and the password exists only in memory for the moment it is needed.
