Need to merge two PDFs or make one smaller? Search the web and you will find dozens of free sites that will do it. You upload the file, wait, and download the result. It is convenient, and that convenience hides a question worth asking: where did your file just go?

## What happens when you upload a PDF

When you use a typical online PDF service, your document is sent to its servers, processed there and sent back. Even when a service promises to delete files after a few hours, you are trusting:

- that the files really are deleted, including backups and logs,
- that the service and its subcontractors are well secured,
- that its staff cannot access your files, and
- that its business model does not depend on your data in some way you did not notice.

For a restaurant menu, that is probably fine. For a passport scan, a signed contract, medical results, payslips or a bank statement, it is a much bigger ask.

## The hidden costs of "free"

Many online PDF sites are free up to a point. Common catches include:

- **Daily limits** on how many tasks you can run.
- **File size caps** that push you towards a paid plan.
- **Watermarks** stamped on your documents.
- **Accounts** you have to create, with your email added to marketing lists.
- **Ads** that slow the page down and track you across the web.

## The alternative: process PDFs on your own computer

Modern browsers are powerful enough to do all of this locally. Unique PDF is a free Chrome extension that runs every tool inside your browser tab. Your files are read from your computer, processed on your computer and saved to your computer. They are never uploaded.

That design has practical benefits beyond privacy:

- **Speed.** No upload or download wait, which makes a real difference with large files.
- **Offline use.** It works on a plane, on a train or anywhere without a connection.
- **No limits.** No daily caps, no watermarks and no account.
- **Strong protection by design.** The extension asks Chrome for only one permission, storing your settings, and it has no access to the websites you visit.

Before uploading a document to any website, ask yourself: would I be comfortable emailing this file to a stranger? If the answer is no, use a tool that keeps it on your computer.
