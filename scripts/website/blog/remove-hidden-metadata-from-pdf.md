Every PDF carries information you do not see on the page: who created it, which software was used, when it was made and changed, and sometimes a title, subject or keywords. This metadata is useful for organising files, but it can also reveal more than you intend, such as an author's full name or an internal project name.

## What PDF metadata can contain

- **Title, author, subject and keywords**, often filled in automatically from your computer or the original document.
- **Creator and producer**: the software that made the file.
- **Creation and modification dates.**
- **XMP metadata**, a second, more detailed copy of this information embedded in the file, and sometimes inside images too.

## How to view and edit metadata

1. **Open Edit metadata** from the Unique PDF quick menu or the Security section.
2. **Add your PDF.** The current values appear in a form.
3. **Change any field**, such as the title or author, or clear the ones you do not want.
4. **Click Save metadata** and download the updated copy.

Unique PDF keeps the visible fields and the XMP copy in sync, so the old values do not linger in one place while you changed them in the other.

## How to remove all metadata

In Edit metadata, choose **Remove all metadata** to clear every field in one step. It also removes XMP metadata attached to images, fonts and other parts of the file, which simpler tools often miss.

## When should you remove metadata?

- Before publishing a PDF on a website.
- Before sending a document to people outside your organisation.
- When sharing anonymised material, such as a report for review.
- Together with redaction: [Redact PDF](/blog/redact-a-pdf-properly) can remove hidden information such as metadata, bookmarks and attachments as part of redacting.

## Private by design

Metadata often contains exactly the details you want to keep private, so it makes no sense to upload the file to clean it. Unique PDF reads and rewrites the document on your own computer.
